REVIEW OF THE LOGIC

L1.  b_10(X') = 2.   Sound; needs the precise citation.
  - The degree n*d/2 - n - 1 = 4 is the right one. I checked it against the source: Dimca's preprint MPI/89-6,
    "Betti Numbers of Hypersurfaces and Defects of Linear Systems" (Duke Math. J. 60, 1990), where for
    dim V = 2m-1 the linear system has degree mN - (sum of weights); for P^10 and cubics that is 5*3 - 11 = 4.
    https://archive.mpim-bonn.mpg.de/4028/1/preprint_1989_6.pdf
    I also sanity-checked the formula on known cases (general 6-nodal cubic threefold: linear forms, defect 1,
    b_4 = 2; three lines in P^2: constants, defect 2, b_2 = 3).
  - Small point to state: the theorem is about quartics of P^10, E4 is about quartics of P^8. The nodes lie in
    P^8 and restriction of quartics is surjective, so the number of conditions is the same (71).
  - The theorem gives the primitive part of H^10; b_10 = 1 + 1 = 2, and H_10 is dual over QQ.
  - Numerology consistent: b_9(X') = 682 - 72 + 1 = 611, chi(X') = 11 - 611 = -600 = -672 + 72.

L2.  The extra class is of Hodge type.   Sound, standard.
  Filling in the step: the kernel of H_10(X') -> sum of H_10(X', X'-p) is the image of H_10(X' - Sigma), which is
  H^8_c(X' - Sigma) = H^8(X') = QQ (Lefschetz), spanned by a linear section missing Sigma, of degree 3. So the
  kernel is a line of nonzero degree and the degree-0 line injects. The local group is H~_9 of the link of a
  9-dimensional node, which is QQ; it is Tate because it is built from the cohomology of a smooth 8-dimensional
  quadric. All maps are morphisms of mixed Hodge structures. A textbook reference for the mixed Hodge structure
  on local cohomology (Peters-Steenbrink, or Dimca's book) is enough.

L3.  H_10(X') -> H_10(X) injective.   Conclusion correct; the argument as written has two unstated steps.
  (a) "Fibre closures smooth along the hyperplane at infinity" is NOT the usual transversality hypothesis: every
      closure meets the hyperplane at infinity in X', which is singular, so the closures are tangent to infinity at
      the 72 nodes. The claim "only affine critical values are atypical" is still true here, but it needs the
      following: where f_2 != 0 one can solve the equation of the total space for the coordinate at infinity, so
      near infinity the pair (total space, divisor at infinity) is a product {f_3 = 0} x C_t with t the projection;
      then Thom's isotopy lemma applies. I checked this by hand; it is the setting of Siersma-Tibar (Duke 1995).
  (b) "0 is not atypical, so H^9(X - X') = 0" also needs that the general fibre has no cohomology in degree 9,
      i.e. that the affine critical points of f are ISOLATED. Not stated. It is true: Z (above) meets infinity
      in the 72 nodes, and at each of them Z is a reduced isolated point exactly because Phi2 != 0 there (bordered
      Hessian has rank 11), so Z has no curve component; 2048 - 72 = 1976 affine critical points with multiplicity.
      Confirmed mod 32003. Euler characteristic agrees: chi(X) - chi(X') = 1377 + 600 = 1977 = 1 + 1976.
  Cleaner route, which I recommend citing instead: Dimca-Paunescu, "On the connectivity of complex affine
  hypersurfaces II" (arXiv math/9906064, Topology 2000). With S(f) the affine cone {grad f_d = 0, f_e = 0}, their
  theorem makes every fibre of f on C^(n+1) (n-1-dim S(f))-connected. Here n = 10 and S(f) = {0} by E3+E5, so
  X - X' = f^-1(0) is 9-connected and H^9 = 0 directly, with no discussion of atypical values.
  https://arxiv.org/abs/math/9906064
  Lefschetz duality H_11(X,X') = H^9(X - X') and the exact sequence are standard.
  Dimension count closes: 2 - 1367 + 1976 - 611 = 0.

L4.  Rational (5,5) class on X not a multiple of h^5.   Sound, standard.
  The image of the degree-0 class is nonzero (L3), rational, of type (5,5) (morphism of mixed Hodge structures
  from a Tate structure), and has degree 0 against h^5 by the projection formula, while h^5 has degree 3.
  This says nothing about algebraicity, and I make no statement about the Hodge conjecture.
